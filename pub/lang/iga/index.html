Inductive Grounded Arithmetic: Goodstein, the Hydra, and Γ0

Bryan Ford
arXiv preprint 2610.34548
October 25, 2026

Abstract:

What does a recursive definition mean when the logic is grounded, so that a sentence is true only if a terminating process makes it so? Kripke’s answer for the special case of truth was the least fixed point. This paper takes that answer as a rule. Reflective Grounded Arithmetic (RGA) already writes any recursive definition as one self-referential term, paradoxes included. Inductive Grounded Arithmetic (IGA) adds the elimination rule for every positive one, so that accessibility, the winning positions of a game, and grounded truth itself each come with their own induction principle. The rule is sound for the omega model and the system is consistent, both machine-checked in Isabelle/HOL. With the rule, IGA proves Goodstein’s theorem outright, in a logic without implication at an undecided hypothesis, and the Kirby–Paris hydra game for primitive recursive strategies, both with every step of the arithmetic a grounded derivation. It proves the Cantor normal forms well-founded, from which the well-foundedness of the standard ε0 notation system in HOL follows, and it certifies the Veblen notation as an ordinal below every φ(n,0). It defines Kripke truth for RGA’s own language as one fixed point that agrees with the omega model exactly. Two equivalences locate what the classical world has and the grounded one does not. IGA proves the whole Veblen notation well-founded exactly when it admits bivalence of accessibility for that order, and bivalence of accessibility for an order is sound exactly when the order is well-founded; so Γ0 lies at most one instance of excluded middle away, at the accessibility predicate of the notation, and that instance, the well-foundedness sentence and one membership claim in a primitive-recursive form of Kleene’s 𝒪 are interderivable over IGA. Conditional proof at arbitrary hypotheses, on the other hand, is inconsistent, by Curry’s paradox. In the semantic sense of the characterization theorem the one rule makes every positive definition an inductive definition, and it shows precisely where implication at a hypothesis that is not provably decided is what has to be paid for.
